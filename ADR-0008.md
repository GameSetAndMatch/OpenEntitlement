# ADR-0008: A Workspace sells one or more Products

- **Status:** accepted; in progress on the `service/python` branch (Products, per-Product roles and the Product lifecycle: OPE-38; Members, invitations and the Cloud service tier: OPE-44). Supersedes the sentence "A Workspace owns one Privilege Catalog" in [ADR-0005](ADR-0005.md) and the Manager-authority scenario in [RFC-0004](docs/rfcs/0004-mcp-server.md).
- **Date:** 2026-11-12

A business can sell several products, for example Analytics and Storage. Some Privileges make no sense in another product's Tiers, and a Group cannot prevent that: a Group only organizes Privileges.

**Decision.** A Workspace contains one or more **Products**.

- Each Product has its own Privilege Catalog, with its own Groups and Tags, and its own Tiers and Tier variants. A Tier can use only its Product's Privileges.
- A Privilege key is unique within its Product. `sso` in Analytics and `sso` in Storage are two separate Privileges.
- An Organization is one customer of the Workspace. For each Product it buys, it has one Tier variant, and its Organization terms belong to that variant.
- The Workspace remains the customer that OpenEntitlement bills. It has one Cloud service tier for all its Products. Cloud Pro allows one Product; Cloud Enterprise allows any number.
- Roles are per Product: a member can be Manager in Analytics and User in Storage, and sees only the Products where they have a role. A Product's Manager edits its Catalog, Tiers, Organization terms and API keys, and invites its Managers and Users. This supersedes RFC-0004's Manager-authority scenario: through MCP too, a Manager may preview and apply Organization-term changes for the Products they manage. A Product's User reads it. An Owner is Owner of the whole Workspace: every Product, creating Products, inviting Owners and the Cloud service tier.
- Every member sees the Organizations' names, so a Product's Manager can give an existing customer a Tier. An Organization shows only the Products where the member has a role. Any Product's Manager can create an Organization.
- The Audit log and the Members page are filtered the same way: a Product's Manager sees their Products, an Owner sees everything.
- A Tier or Product that a customer uses cannot be deleted. It can be archived: it keeps serving its current customers, with all its variants, but cannot be given to a new customer, an archived Product gets no new API keys, and an archived Tier is never suggested as an upgrade. It can be restored. One that no customer uses can be deleted.
- A Workspace cannot move from Cloud Enterprise to Cloud Pro while it has more than one Product. Self-hosted installs have no limit.
- An API key belongs to one Product. An evaluation request names that Product, and the service rejects a request that names another one. The protocol is proposed in [RFC-0005](docs/rfcs/0005-product-scoped-evaluation.md).
- The console shows a Product selector at the top of the sidebar. Catalog and Tiers show the selected Product. Organizations and Settings cover the whole Workspace. With a single Product, the selector is hidden.

## Implementation notes

These clarify how the decision is carried out; they do not change it.

- **Product ID.** A Product's key (`^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$`) is its identifier in evaluation requests and never changes; its name can. A Workspace's first Product takes the Workspace's name, with a key derived from it (`Acme analytics` → `acme_analytics`).
- **Who manages Products.** Owners (and Platform administrators) create, rename, archive, restore and delete Products. A Workspace keeps at least one Product, so its last one cannot be deleted.
- **Tiers (OPE-40).** A Product's Manager edits its Tiers and Tier variants. Creating a Tier saves it with its standard variant in one transaction. An archived Tier is never suggested as an upgrade and cannot be newly chosen as a status-policy fallback; its variants stay editable. A status-policy field whose fallback already pointed at the Tier before it was archived keeps it unchanged when the policy is saved (the archived Tier keeps serving), but once that field changes the archived variant cannot be chosen again. A Tier that a status-policy fallback uses cannot be deleted until the policy changes.
- **Status policy per Product.** Each Product has its own status policy, because its fallback is a Tier variant of that Product and values from two Products are never combined.
- **A member's Role shown for the whole Workspace** (in the Workspace switcher) is their highest one: Owner, then Manager, then User.
- **Members and invitations (OPE-44).** A Product's Managers change its members' Roles between Manager, User and none, and remove members from the Products they manage; a member left with no Role, by either action, leaves the Workspace. Only Owners invite, appoint, change or remove Owners, and a Workspace keeps at least one. Making a member Owner drops their Product Roles; giving an Owner a Product Role ends their being Owner. An invitation names one Product (or none for an Owner) and the invited email; it works once, for seven days. Accepting it never changes a Role the Account already has in that Product or as Owner: it only adds the invited Product Role to a member who has none there.
- **Cloud service tier (OPE-44).** Only a Platform administrator changes it, and only on OpenEntitlement Cloud (`OE_DEPLOYMENT_MODE=saas`); a Workspace starts on Cloud Pro. Archived Products count toward the limit, because they still serve customers. A refused downgrade lists every Product, since which one to keep is the customer's choice.

## Considered options

- **One Workspace Catalog, where each Product picks the Privileges it offers.** Rejected because one Privilege would then have one default but values from several Products. An Organization on Analytics Pro and Storage Basic would need a rule to combine them.
- **One Workspace per product.** Rejected because the business would have separate members, Organizations and billing for each of its products.
- **Workspace-wide roles only.** Rejected because a business usually has separate teams per product, and one product's team should not change or see another product's Catalog, Tiers or terms.
- **Using Groups.** Rejected because Groups organize Privileges but never restrict where a Privilege can be used.

## Consequences

- No Workspace exists yet, so nothing is migrated. Every new Workspace starts with one Product.
- Evaluation always happens within one Product. Values from two Products are never combined.
